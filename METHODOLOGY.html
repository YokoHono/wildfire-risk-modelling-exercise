Wildfire Property Risk — Methodology and Submission Report

UL Wildfire Risk Modeling Exercise (2026), OroraTech submission.
Scenarios: Town of Forests (Lake Tahoe, CA — mountainous WUI / Intermix), Town of Prairies (Amarillo, TX — flat grassland WUI).

1. Overview

Every building gets one risk score in [0, 1], built from the five components the exercise manual defines: hazard, vulnerability, exposure, severity of consequence, and capacity to withstand impact. The first four amplify expected loss; capacity dampens it. 

risk_gross = (Hazard × Vulnerability × Exposure × Severity) ^ (1/4)
risk_net   = risk_gross × (1 − 0.5 × Capacity)
risk_score = min-max rescaled to [0, 1] within this scenario

The fourth root keeps the multiplicative semantics of the catastrophe-modelling identity (a building scores high only when all four amplifiers contribute) while keeping absolute numbers in a range that's comfortable to read on a map. Capacity dampens rather than caps, so a fully resilient property has its gross risk halved at most, not zeroed.

Forest_submission.ipynb and Prairie_submission.ipynb are the canonical implementation: every cell maps to one step of the pipeline, §2b documents the inputs, §2c lists the synthetic fields we don't consume (with reasons), and §6c carries the output schema. This document is the higher-level story plus the information evaluators need to interpret and locate the deliverables.

2. Inputs

The synthetic exercise data is used as provided. Four real-world datasets back specific components:

DatasetUsed for
CAL FIRE DINS damage inspections (~95k fire records) training the vulnerability regressor
MTBS burn perimeters training labels for the climate hazard classifier
FPA-FOD ignitions (1.88M, 1992–2015) density features for climate hazard
USFS WUI 2020 (Radeloff / SILVIS Lab) exposure modifier for WUI-overlapping parcels

Both scenarios also use OroraTech's own coupled fire-spread simulation as the scenario-hazard input. The wildland front is carried by ForeFire (Filippi et al. 2010), a discrete-event front-tracking solver running the Rothermel (1972) surface fire-spread model on the LANDFIRE-derived fuel / terrain / moisture rasters. We couple a WUI structure-ignition overlay implementing the Purnomo et al. (2024 / 2025) semi-physical level-set model on top of ForeFire — direct flame contact, point-source radiation, lognormal-PDF embers from Sardoy et al. (2008), and the cumulative flux-time-product ignition criterion of Lee (2009). The overlay implementation lives at structure_overlay.py. For each scenario the simulation produces 73 cumulative wildland-perimeter slices over the 12 h scenario plus a per-structure ignition timestamp wherever the fire reaches a building (1,129 structures in Forest, 1,039 in Prairie).

3. Components

3.1 Hazard

"Did fire actually reach this building in the scenario?"

For each scenario we read the per-property ignition timestamps from the OroraTech sim and map them to a score with hazard_sim = max(0, 1 − arrival_hours / 12). A building ignited in the first hour gets ~0.9; one that doesn't ignite gets 0.

To avoid relying only on a single sim run, we also train a gradient-boosted classifier on the MTBS burn perimeter that overlaps each scenario's extent (Angora 2007 for Forest, Stone Ridge 2011 for Prairie), using LANDFIRE-derived rasters and FPA-FOD ignition density as features. This gives every building a non-zero baseline hazard_climate.

We combine the two with hazard = max(hazard_climate, hazard_sim) rather than a 50/50 blend. Averaging would halve the sim signal for ignited buildings, given how close to zero the MTBS classifier scores the bulk of the area. Trained climate-hazard classifier AUC: 0.95 (Forest), 0.99 (Prairie).

3.2 Vulnerability

A HistGradientBoostingRegressor trained on ~95k fire-only structural records from CAL FIRE DINS, predicting damage severity on a [0, 1] scale (destroyed = 1, no damage = 0). The 22 features mirror the synthetic schema: roof type, siding, eaves, vent screens, windows, deck, fence, year built, property value.

Held-out performance: MAE ≈ 0.28, R² ≈ 0.47. Good enough for relative ranking, not calibrated for absolute damage probabilities. Defensible-space compliance reduces the raw output by up to 40 % (vulnerability = raw × (1 − 0.4 × mean(zone_0, zone_1, zone_2))).

3.3 Exposure

What's physically at stake if a building is lost. Three rank-normalised sub-scores: asset value (50 %), footprint × usage weight (30 %), and adjacency (20 %). Buildings whose polygon intersects a USFS WUI 2020 Intermix or Interface block get a 1.15× modifier.

3.4 Severity of consequence

Loss-of-life and service-disruption magnitude. We weight occupancy load (sqft × stories) by usage type — residential 1.0–1.4, mixed 1.2, commercial 0.6, industrial 0.5 — and rank-normalise. This is a separate axis from exposure: exposure says "this asset is worth $X"; severity says "losing this asset hurts in non-financial ways too".

3.5 Capacity to withstand impact

The dampening component. Five resilience sub-scores blended together with the weights below; the weighted sum is then applied as risk_net = risk_gross × (1 − 0.5 × capacity), so a perfect-capacity building has its gross risk halved.

Sub-scoreWeightHow it's computed
cap_defensible_space0.35 Mean of zone_0_compliant, zone_1_defensible, zone_2_defensible — 1 if all three zones are compliant.
cap_construction_quality0.25 Weighted sum of year_built_2000_2025 (modern code), siding_non_combust + roof_non_combust (non-combustible envelope), and siding_fire_resist (fire-resistant facade).
cap_ember_resistance0.20 Weighted sum of fine vent screens (ventscreens_1_8 > ventscreens_1_4 > ventscreens_1_2), tempered windows (window_*_tempered), and enclosed eaves (eaves_enclosed).
cap_access0.15 exp(−dist_to_road_m / 60). Closer to a road → faster response and evacuation; falls off with a ~60 m e-folding length.
cap_wui_maintenance0.05 Rank-normalised acres_treated_wui (0.5 if null).

4. Output deliverables

Everything submission-relevant lives in outputs/. Filenames are scenario-prefixed (Forest_… / Prairie_…).

  • <scenario>_risk_scored_5component.geojson — the headline deliverable. Every building polygon plus the five component scores, the five capacity sub-scores, and the final risk, alongside the full set of synthetic input attributes for auditing how a score was reached. Schema in §5. 108 columns (Forest) / 99 columns (Prairie).
  • <scenario>_risk_arcgis.geojson — the same building polygons trimmed to the 51 most useful columns (all component / capacity / risk scores plus the construction, defensible-space and adjacency inputs an evaluator might want to filter on). This is the file uploaded to ArcGIS Online (§4.1); see make_arcgis_outputs.py for the exact column list.
  • <scenario>_risk_components.html — interactive folium map with seven togglable layers: top-10 highlighted, then each component and the final risk. Per-layer adaptive colour scale so bimodal distributions (Forest hazard) read clearly.
  • <scenario>_risk_components_raw.html — same map with a fixed vmin = 0 scale on every layer, for honest side-by-side comparison without the adaptive stretch.
  • <scenario>_risk_map.html — single-layer interactive map of the final score.
  • <scenario>_risk_scored.geojson — 3-component intermediate that run.py writes; the notebooks build the 5-component score on top.

The wildland surface-fire spread itself is also published as a separate GeoJSON per scenario, so evaluators can inspect or overlay the fire front independently of the per-building scoring:

  • data_cache/fire_spread_simulations/forest_scenario/forecast_forest_wui.geojson and data_cache/fire_spread_simulations/prairie_scenario/forecast_prairie_wui_minSpeed0015.geojson — 73 cumulative wildland-perimeter slices over the 12 h scenario, one MultiPolygon per slice with properties date (ISO timestamp) and area_sqkm (cumulative burned area in square kilometres). EPSG:4326.
  • forecast_<scenario>_properties.geojson in the same folder — per-structure ignition timestamps for every building the fire reaches (1,129 in Forest, 1,039 in Prairie). Properties include bldgid, usage, property_value, ignition (ISO timestamp), and arrival_hours_from_ignition. The same arrival times drive the hazard_sim column in the 5-component output.

4.1 Esri-compatible Feature Service (per manual §2b)

The slimmed <scenario>_risk_arcgis.geojson file (51 columns, the column set listed in make_arcgis_outputs.py) is uploaded to ArcGIS Online as a hosted feature service and shared into the dedicated exercise Group, view-only as required by §2b. Layer naming convention: OroraTech_<Scenario>_RiskScore. The layer's field schema is a subset of §5 below — all score columns and the audit-useful inputs are kept; the 50+ raw inputs that only feed the classifiers (raster samples, treelist features, ignition densities, string duplicates of binary flags) are dropped. Evaluators wanting a colour ramp on the headline score can configure graduated colours on risk_score (0–1) in the layer's visualisation settings, or swap in any of the component columns (hazard, vulnerability, exposure, severity, capacity) to symbolise on that axis instead.

6. Reading the score

A value of risk_score near 1.0 means the property is in the worst combination of all four amplifiers (fire reaches it, it's vulnerable, it's high-value with dense neighbours, losing it has occupancy or service consequences) with little resilience to absorb the loss. A value near 0 means at least one component is essentially zero — either fire never reaches the property, or its construction is fire-resistant, or its replacement value and occupancy are negligible, or its resilience is high enough to halve an otherwise high score.

risk_score is rescaled per scenario, so 0.7 in Forest is comparable to another 0.7 in Forest but not directly comparable across to Prairie.

8. Intended use

Suitable for community wildfire protection planning, mitigation prioritisation, pre-incident response planning, resource allocation, and insurance portfolio triage. For mitigation specifically, the cap_* sub-scores point at which mitigation would most help on a given parcel — defensible-space inspection, structural hardening, ember-resistant vents, better road access, or fuel-break maintenance.

Not appropriate for insurance pricing, life-safety alerts to individual residents, or cross-scenario comparison (the rescale is per-scenario).

9. Validation

Per the manual §6.1.3.f, the model is not formally validated against historical losses. The vulnerability regressor reports MAE ≈ 0.28 and R² ≈ 0.47 on a held-out 20 % split of DINS records — usable for relative ranking, not for absolute damage-probability claims. The climate hazard classifier reports AUC ≈ 0.95 (Forest) and 0.99 (Prairie) on held-out grid points, but each scenario is in-sample to a single historical fire. The OroraTech sim is the authoritative scenario hazard; we treat its arrival timestamps as ground truth for hazard_sim.

10. Reproducibility

Python 3.11 (3.9 also works), ~4 GB RAM, no GPU. End-to-end from cached artefacts: about 2 minutes per notebook on Forest, 1 minute on Prairie. Cold start (no caches) adds ~15 minutes for the upstream Monte Carlo simulation in run.py and 5–10 minutes for the one-off DINS / MTBS / FPA-FOD / WUI downloads. The OroraTech fire-spread simulation runs separately; its outputs are committed in data_cache/fire_spread_simulations/. 

bash install_packages.sh                          # build .venv, install deps
source .venv/bin/activate
python run.py --scenario both                     # 3-component intermediate
python extract_property_ignitions.py              # per-property ignitions
jupyter trust Forest_submission.ipynb Prairie_submission.ipynb
jupyter notebook                                  # run all cells in each

11. Limitations

The vulnerability model captures construction-level damage probability. It doesn't say anything about contents loss, post-fire structural integrity, or occupant exposure.

The climate hazard model is trained on a single historical fire per site, so it represents the spatial pattern of that burn, not all possible futures. Useful in combination with the sim, not as a standalone prior.

The OroraTech sim is deterministic for the provided ignition. We don't run it under perturbed wind / moisture to get an ignition probability; we treat the arrival time as ground truth for hazard_sim.

FPA-FOD stops at 2015. Post-2015 ignition trends (substantially different in California) aren't in the features.

The aggregation is rescaled per scenario, so risk_score isn't directly comparable across Forest ↔ Prairie. Compare within a scenario only.

No formal validation against historical losses. Appropriate for relative ranking and mitigation prioritisation, not for insurance pricing.
